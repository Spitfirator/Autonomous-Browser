\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

\section{Component evidence}

Before interpreting agent behaviour, the evaluation first checks that the
configured guards behaved as intended. The model-free AgentDojo replay covered
7,404 security cases across 16 non-manual attack families. Every case was
detected and received the configured hard-block outcome. None of the 97
distinct benign tasks was hard-blocked, although one reached the approval
threshold. A separate detector-v2 scorecard then tested the exact text
representations produced by both adapters. Recall was 1.000 with no false
positives on the two AgentDojo corpora. On the WASP development corpus, recall
was 1.000 and precision was 0.988, with one false positive among 39 benign
pages. These WASP figures are detector-development evidence, not outcomes from
the final three-seed browser experiment. Detailed component and action-gating
results appear in \cref{app:assurance}.

Together, the replay and scorecard establish two conditions for interpreting
the end-to-end results. First, the configured policy produced its intended
decision for every defined security case without hard-blocking the benign task
set. Second, the detector was tested on the text produced by the AgentDojo and
WASP adapters, not only on the original benchmark payloads. However, these
remain development-regression results because the rules were refined against
the same corpora; they do not estimate performance on unseen attacks. Their
narrower value is to confirm that later failures cannot be explained simply by
a detector that never fired or a policy fixture that failed to load. The
following sections can therefore examine whether the complete governed
configuration also preserved useful agent trajectories.

The averages, however, conceal movement in both directions at the task level.
For 4B, enforcement added 84 useful outcomes, removed 206 attacker successes,
and added 149 useful-and-secure outcomes. With 9B, it added 45 useful outcomes
and prevented 130 attacker successes; across matched tasks, 92 failures became
successes while 47 successes were lost. Utility for 27B rose by 55 and secure
utility by 138, but that net change comprised 84 gains and 29 losses. An
aggregate improvement did not therefore mean that every trajectory benefited.

Secure utility matters here because user success could coexist with
compromise. In off mode, this occurred in 65, 55, and 83 episodes for 4B, 9B,
and 27B; enforcement reduced all three counts to zero. Conversely, failing the
user task was not a proxy for safety: compromised failures changed from 141 to
zero, from 76 to one, and from 120 to zero. The four-way outcome split thus
exposes risk that either utility or attacker success alone would conceal.

Utility changes were also uneven across task suites. For 4B, utility increased
in banking (86 to 99), Slack (58 to 60), travel (35 to 100), and workspace (183
to 187), while attacker success fell to zero in every suite. By comparison,
the 27B profile fell in banking (118 to 99), remained unchanged in Slack (70),
and rose in travel (76 to 112) and workspace (164 to 202). The aggregate should
therefore not be read as a uniform utility improvement.

The telemetry also shows how Sentinel intervened. It rarely stopped actions
outright. Detector-positive tool output appeared in 600, 615, and 609 enforce
cases for 4B, 9B, and 27B, while each model recorded only nine explicit action
blocks and no approval actions. In the reviewed 27B aggregate, 2,181 actions
were allowed and all nine blocks invoked
\texttt{sensitive\_data\_flow}. These records identify sanitisation before the
next decision as the main intervention, with action policy as a backstop.

This intervention pattern offers a plausible explanation for the joint
security and utility gains. Indirect injection can pull the agent away from the
user's task or consume its step budget. Once detected malicious content is
removed, the planner has an opportunity to resume the original trajectory
without carrying the attack into its next decision. All three model rows are
consistent with this account, but they remain independent matched comparisons
and do not establish a model-size trend.

Clean runs provide a final reference point. Clean utility remained above
attacked-off utility for every model: 78.4\% versus 57.6\% for 4B, 82.5\%
versus 67.1\% for 9B, and 83.5\% versus 68.0\% for 27B. Enforcement did not
return any model to its clean ceiling, but it recovered part of the gap while
suppressing attacker outcomes. These comparisons reinforce the need to report
clean capability, attacked utility, and secure utility together; an ASR
reduction alone would hide the distinction.

\subsection{What the policy-only/full contrast establishes}

The policy-only condition retains boundary neutralisation, the detector
warning, and action policy while disabling browser sanitisation. Relative to
off, it reduced audited attacker success from 17/252 to 1/252 and
attacker-directed execution from 42/252 to 15/252. This is a combined system
effect: it cannot be attributed to action policy alone.

Full differs only by enabling the configured sanitisation treatment. Aggregate
utility remained 149/252, while attacker-directed attempts and executions
changed from 15/252 to 0/252. The decrement was five episodes in every seed.
Audited attacker success changed from 1/252 to 0/252 and secure utility from
148/252 to 149/252. The terminal difference is numerically small because the
policy-only agent completed only one attacker goal, but the trajectory
difference is not: no matching attacker-directed proposal was recorded under
full enforcement in 252 episodes.

Mechanism telemetry supports that interpretation. Full enforcement performed
740 sanitisation attempts and confirmed 740 removals, comprising 21 direct-line
and 719 bounded-window cases. None remained detector-positive, none was
unattributable, and no post-removal warning was emitted. Policy-only deliberately
recorded no sanitisation events. It raised two approval requests in one episode;
scripted no-participant resolution stopped one attacker-directed action and one
non-attacker-directed action. The latter is the single potential false block in
\cref{tab:wasp-27b}. Full generated no attacker-directed proposal and raised no
approval, block, handover, or security-pause event.

No human participated in these campaigns, so actual human intervention is
not applicable. Pre-execution mediation averaged below 0.6 milliseconds per
governed decision in every condition, with no recorded human waiting. End-to-end
episode duration was lower under enforcement, but this is a system-level
trajectory outcome and cannot be interpreted as middleware overhead alone.

\subsection{Security by incompetence}

Terminal ASR alone substantially understates baseline diversion. Of the 252 off
episodes, 42 executed an attacker-directed action, but only 17 completed the
attacker goal. The remaining 25 directly demonstrate execution without terminal
success: the browser acted for the attacker, yet the agent failed to finish.
One further episode proposed but did not execute a matching action. The other
209 contained no recorded attempt. They are diagnostically indeterminate, not
evidence of incapability, because exposure, recognition, navigation, replanning,
or early termination may have failed first.

Full enforcement recorded neither an attempted nor an executed attacker action.
Zero terminal successes in 252 episodes does not establish a zero underlying
rate: the rule-of-three heuristic gives an approximate upper bound of 1.19\%,
and the repeated benchmark matrix is not a random deployment sample. The
stronger evidence is the agreement of native outcome, independent action audit,
per-seed replication, and 740 verified removals.

\subsection{Campaign integrity}

All 756 final episodes received native scores. Every cell matched its expected
seed, condition, generated inputs, model, policy, and middleware record, and no
controller restart, incomplete checkpoint, provenance mismatch, task error, or
structured-output failure entered the denominators. The website was reset
before every cell. The earlier 9B runs informed development but are excluded
from this comparison because their model and middleware provenance changed.
Finally, the controller used the same cell sequence throughout; this supports
operational consistency but cannot rule out a time-dependent order effect.

\section{Result synthesis}

Taken together, the three evidence levels tell a consistent story without being
pooled. Component replay verifies the configured guard contracts, AgentDojo
measures native outcomes in controlled tool environments, and WASP tests the
same enforcement pattern in a browser-native loop. Nevertheless, security has
to be read alongside utility and paired trajectories. The next chapter
interprets that joint evidence, including the configured sanitisation
treatment and the limits of the resulting claim.
