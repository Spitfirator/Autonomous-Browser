\chapter{Results}
\label{chap:results}

\section{Component evidence}

The model-free AgentDojo replay covered 16 non-manual attack families and
7,404 security cases. All defined attack cases were detected and received the
configured hard-block outcome. None of the 97 distinct benign tasks was
blocked; one reached the approval threshold. The detector-v2 regression
scorecard recorded recall 1.000 with no false positives on both AgentDojo
corpora. On the WASP development corpus it recorded recall 1.000, precision
0.988, and one false positive among 39 benign pages. Full component and
action-gating results appear in \cref{app:assurance}; the end-to-end results
below determine whether those mechanisms preserved useful trajectories.

The replay establishes two prerequisites for interpreting the agent results.
First, the configured policy produced the intended decision for every defined
security case without obtaining that coverage by blocking the benign task set.
Second, the detector operated on the representations actually supplied by the
two adapters, not an abstract list of attack strings. The near-
perfect component scores are not treated as a generalisation estimate because
the rules were refined against these corpora. Their role is narrower: they show
that a failure in an end-to-end row is not explained by the detector simply
being disabled or the policy fixture failing to load.

For 4B, enforcement produced 84 additional useful outcomes, 206 fewer
attacker successes, and 149 additional useful-and-secure outcomes. The 9B row
produced 45 additional useful outcomes and prevented 130 attacker successes;
paired transitions contained 92 utility gains and 47 losses. For 27B, utility
increased by 55 cases, all 203 off-condition attacker successes were absent,
and secure utility increased by 138. Its paired outcomes contained 84 utility
gains and 29 losses, demonstrating that the aggregate gain was substantial but
not cost-free for every trajectory.

The joint outcomes make the security gain more concrete. Enforcement removed
65, 55, and 83 cases in which the user and attacker oracles both succeeded for
4B, 9B, and 27B respectively. These are especially important: apparent task
completion could otherwise conceal an unauthorised side effect. It also removed
141, 76, and 120 attacker successes from trajectories that had already failed
the user, showing why unsuccessful work cannot be counted as inherently safe.
After enforcement, every successful 4B and 27B user outcome was secure; the 9B
row retained one attacker success in an unsuccessful user trajectory.

Suite-level outcomes show that the aggregate was not generated by one task
environment. For 4B, native utility increased in banking (86 to 99), Slack (58
to 60), travel (35 to 100), and workspace (183 to 187), while attacker success
fell to zero in every suite. The 27B profile was more heterogeneous: utility
fell in banking (118 to 99), was unchanged in Slack (70), and rose in travel
(76 to 112) and workspace (164 to 202). That variation is why the analysis
retains paired task outcomes and limitations; the aggregate should not be read
as a uniform utility improvement.

The mechanism was selective and rarely relied on refusal. Detector-positive tool
output occurred in 600, 615, and 609 enforce cases for 4B, 9B, and 27B. Each
model recorded nine explicit action blocks and no approval actions. In the
reviewed 27B aggregate, 2,181 actions were allowed and all nine blocks were
\texttt{sensitive\_data\_flow}. Sanitising untrusted tool results before the
next decision was therefore the dominant recorded intervention; action policy
served as a backstop.

The utility gains follow from the structure of indirect injection. A diverted
agent may abandon the user's task or consume its step budget. Removing the
malicious tool result can restore the original trajectory as well as prevent
the attacker goal. This effect appears in each independent model row and is
interpreted as a paired enforcement outcome, not as a model-size trend.

Clean utility remained above attacked-off utility for every model: 78.4\%
versus 57.6\% for 4B, 82.5\% versus 67.1\% for 9B, and 83.5\% versus 68.0\% for
27B. Enforce utility did not exceed each clean ceiling, but it closed much of
the gap while suppressing attacker outcomes. This is the expected direction if
injected tool results cause both security violations and task diversion. It is
also why the analysis reports clean capability, attacked utility, and secure
utility separately. An ASR reduction alone would hide these distinctions.

\subsection{What the policy-only/full contrast establishes}

The policy-only condition retains boundary neutralisation, the detector
warning, and action policy while disabling browser sanitisation. Relative to
off, it reduced audited attacker success from 17/252 to 1/252 and
attacker-directed execution from 42/252 to 15/252. This is a combined system
effect: it cannot be attributed to action policy alone.

Full differs only by enabling the configured sanitisation treatment. Aggregate
utility remained 149/252, while attacker-directed attempts and executions
changed from 15/252 to 0/252. The decrement was five episodes in every seed.
Audited attacker success changed from 1/252 to 0/252 and secure utility from
148/252 to 149/252. The terminal difference is numerically small because the
policy-only agent completed only one attacker goal, but the trajectory
difference is not: no matching attacker-directed proposal was recorded under
full enforcement in 252 episodes.

Mechanism telemetry supports that interpretation. Full enforcement performed
740 sanitisation attempts and confirmed 740 removals, comprising 21 direct-line
and 719 bounded-window cases. None remained detector-positive, none was
unattributable, and no post-removal warning was emitted. Policy-only deliberately
recorded no sanitisation events. It raised two approval requests in one episode;
scripted no-participant resolution stopped one attacker-directed action and one
non-attacker-directed action. The latter is the single potential false block in
\cref{tab:wasp-27b}. Full generated no attacker-directed proposal and raised no
approval, block, handover, or security-pause event.

No human participated in these campaigns, so actual human intervention is
not applicable. Pre-execution mediation averaged below 0.6 milliseconds per
governed decision in every condition, with no recorded human waiting. End-to-end
episode duration was lower under enforcement, but this is a system-level
trajectory outcome and cannot be interpreted as middleware overhead alone.

\subsection{Security by incompetence}

Terminal ASR alone substantially understates baseline diversion. Of the 252 off
episodes, 42 executed an attacker-directed action, but only 17 completed the
attacker goal. The remaining 25 directly demonstrate execution without terminal
success: the browser acted for the attacker, yet the agent failed to finish.
One further episode proposed but did not execute a matching action. The other
209 contained no recorded attempt. They are diagnostically indeterminate, not
evidence of incapability, because exposure, recognition, navigation, replanning,
or early termination may have failed first.

Full enforcement recorded neither an attempted nor an executed attacker action.
Zero terminal successes in 252 episodes does not establish a zero underlying
rate: the rule-of-three heuristic gives an approximate upper bound of 1.19\%,
and the repeated benchmark matrix is not a random deployment sample. The
stronger evidence is the agreement of native outcome, independent action audit,
per-seed replication, and 740 verified removals.

\subsection{Campaign integrity}

All 756 final episodes received native scores. Every cell matched its expected
seed, condition, generated inputs, model, policy, and middleware record, and no
controller restart, incomplete checkpoint, provenance mismatch, task error, or
structured-output failure entered the denominators. The website was reset
before every cell. The earlier 9B runs informed development but are excluded
from this comparison because their model and middleware provenance changed.
Finally, the controller used the same cell sequence throughout; this supports
operational consistency but cannot rule out a time-dependent order effect.

\section{Result synthesis}

The three evidence levels align without being pooled. Component replay confirms
the configured guard contracts. Across AgentDojo's controlled tool
environments, enforcement sharply reduced attacker success while utility and
secure utility rose. The replicated WASP study then preserved the security
direction in a browser loop: all 17 audited terminal successes and 42
attacker-executing episodes observed off were absent under full enforcement
across three seeds.

Utility bounds the claim. Full WASP enforcement completed five fewer episodes
than off in aggregate, with gains and losses varying by seed, while secure
utility increased by 11. This supports useful browser transfer, not cost-free
improvement. The policy-only/full ablation locates the contribution of the
configured treatment more precisely: attacker-directed execution remained in
15/252 policy-only episodes, whereas none was proposed under full, at unchanged
aggregate utility.

The combined evidence therefore answers the research question at the level
claimed. Independent enforcement changed both the observations shaping the
planner and the effects reaching the executor, while native oracles and action
audit kept task failure, diversion, execution, and terminal compromise
distinct.
