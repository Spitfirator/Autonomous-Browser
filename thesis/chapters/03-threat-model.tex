\chapter{Threat model and security requirements}
\label{chap:threat-model}

\section{System model}

The operator supplies a trusted goal $G$. At step $t$, the browser produces
an observation $O_t$, the model proposes an action $a_t$, and the executor
may update environment state $S_t$:

\[
  O_t=\mathsf{observe}(S_t), \qquad
  a_t=\mathsf{agent}(G,O_{\leq t},A_{<t}), \qquad
  S_{t+1}=\mathsf{execute}(S_t,a_t).
\]

An ungoverned loop passes the model proposal directly to the executor.
\systemname\ inserts two transformations:

\[
  \widehat{O}_t=\mathsf{guard}_{O}(O_t,P,C_t), \qquad
  d_t=\mathsf{guard}_{A}(a_t,S_t,P,C_t),
\]

where $P$ is live policy and $C_t$ is run-local governance state. The
executor is called only when $d_t$ returns \emph{allow}, including an action
whose narrow approval remains valid after fresh preflight. Block, timeout,
cancellation, unavailable required context, and unresolved authority do not
execute $a_t$. The model is therefore a proposal generator rather than the
source of authority for privileged effects.

\section{Assets and trust boundary}

Protected assets are the operator's task authority, authenticated browser
session, private and security-sensitive data, local files available to the
browser, integrity of remote state, consent decisions, and audit
confidentiality. Availability is also relevant because an agent that never
acts has no useful autonomy, although denial is preferable to an unauthorised
effect when required security context is missing.

The local service, configured policy, middleware, browser automation library,
and browser form the trusted computing base. The model, page, and data returned
by tools are untrusted. The extension communicates with the local service but
is not a reference monitor: pending requests, timers, validation, and execution
remain server-owned.

\section{Adversary}

The primary adversary controls content that an agent encounters while pursuing
a legitimate task. This may be a page, user-generated record, link label,
extracted value, or stored tool result. The adversary knows the general system
design and attempts to replace the operator's goal, impersonate a higher-trust
role, suppress warnings, request disclosure, induce code or file access, or
persist influence into later turns. The action may be conspicuous, such as
cross-origin navigation, or use the same same-origin form controls required by
the legitimate task.

The adversary does not compromise the operating system, browser binary,
Sentinel process, model provider, or local policy file. Direct human page
interaction and browser effects outside the governed BrowserUse tool path are
also outside complete mediation. The evaluated attacks are those instantiated
by AgentDojo and WASP. Adaptive and pixel-only attacks are outside the measured
task matrices; they remain relevant limitations and motivate the future
evaluations identified in \cref{chap:discussion}.

\section{Security requirements}
\label{sec:security-objectives}

Five requirements translate the threat model into testable behaviour.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{R\arabic*:},leftmargin=16mm]
  \item \textbf{Independent and complete action mediation.} Every
  model-proposed privileged action entering the wrapped tool boundary is
  evaluated against live context immediately before execution. Each attempt
  receives exactly one attempted and one terminal audit outcome, including
  errors, denials, timeouts, handovers, and cancellation.

  \item \textbf{Verified observation handling.} Browser, extraction, and
  tool-result content remains untrusted regardless of origin. A detected
  region may be forwarded only after attributable modification and clean
  re-detection of the reconstructed model input. Known residual or
  unattributable current-page content is withheld; bounded extraction or
  historical values are omitted in full.

  \item \textbf{Fail-closed policy and data flow.} Domain blocks, unavailable
  required context, unsafe navigation schemes, and configured hard risk take
  precedence over interactive approval. Recognised protected values cannot be
  typed, scripted, or uploaded by the agent in enforce mode; the human performs
  the step through takeover.

  \item \textbf{Narrow authority.} Approval is explicit, run-local,
  expiring, and bound to the reviewed action, origin, capabilities, taint,
  sensitive-data categories, target, and applicable parameter evidence. Live
  policy and browser context are recomputed before use. Silence, timeout,
  malformed responses, disconnect, and stop never become permission.

  \item \textbf{Privacy-preserving accountability.} Decisions remain
  inspectable through redacted rules, risk factors, outcomes, and run-keyed
  digests. Raw protected values, attacker payloads, screenshot pixels, and
  unrestricted model rationale are excluded from persistent and extension
  telemetry by default.
\end{enumerate}

Together, these requirements define what Sentinel can enforce on the paths it
controls. They do not make every allowed model decision correct: the planner
may still choose an unhelpful action, and the detector may miss an attack. The
evaluation therefore combines contract tests with end-to-end security and
utility outcomes.

\section{Scope of the claim}

Sentinel is evaluated as a local research prototype. Its ordinary interactive
path is DOM/accessibility based; screenshot pixels are withheld because the
implemented observation guard cannot sanitise rendered text. The loopback API
checks permitted origins, but does not isolate the service from another
compromised process running under the same operating-system account.

The dissertation claims neither universal prompt-injection prevention nor
human-factors effectiveness. Its security claim concerns the model-input and
pre-execution paths that Sentinel actually mediates; its utility claim concerns
the reported task suites and native oracles. Evidence must therefore establish
two outcomes together: fewer evaluated violations and continued completion of
legitimate work. Detector accuracy or blanket refusal would not answer the
research question on its own.
