\chapter{Threat model and security requirements}
\label{chap:threat-model}

\section{System model}

The operator begins by supplying a trusted goal $G$. At planning step $t$, the
browser-agent runtime samples browser state $S_t^O$ and forms an ungoverned
observation $O_t$. Once the model input crosses the observation boundary, the
model may propose a finite sequence
$A_t=(a_{t,1},\ldots,a_{t,n_t})$. BrowserUse then attempts an ordered prefix of
that sequence; let $m_t\leq n_t$ denote the number attempted. Each action is
mediated against live state immediately before execution, and $d_{t,i}$ records
the resulting refusal, handoff outcome, or tool result. Histories $\leq t$ and
$<t$ run through and before planning step $t$, respectively:

\[
\begin{aligned}
  O_t &= \mathsf{observe}(S_t^O), &
  M_t &= \mathsf{compose}(G,O_{\leq t},A_{<t},D_{<t}), \\
  \widehat{M}_t &= \mathsf{guard}_{M}(M_t,P_t^O,C_t), &
  A_t &= \mathsf{agent}(\widehat{M}_t), \\
  d_{t,i} &=
  \mathsf{govern}_{A}(a_{t,i},S_{t,i}^A,P_{t,i}^A,C_{t,i}),
  \quad 1\leq i\leq m_t.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here, $D_t=(d_{t,1},\ldots,d_{t,m_t})$. The message $M_t$ combines the trusted
goal with the observation history, earlier proposals, and their outcomes;
$\widehat{M}_t$ is the governed form supplied to the model.
Consequently, untrusted observations and tool-result text cross the same
model-input boundary.

The state symbols show when browser context is read. $S_t^O$ is sampled to
construct the observation, whereas each $S_{t,i}^A$ is sampled immediately
before its action, allowing the page or browser to evolve between planning and
execution. Similarly, $P_t^O$ and $P_{t,i}^A$ are live policy snapshots at the
model and action boundaries, while $C_t$ and $C_{t,i}$ are run-local governance
states. BrowserUse abandons any unattempted suffix after an error, a
sequence-terminating action, or a URL or focus change.

At these complementary boundaries, the model-input guard may sanitise or omit
untrusted regions. It withholds an unresolved current observation until fresh
recapture or fail-closed termination, while the action governor wraps the
executor itself, not merely the policy decision. Off mode disables prompt-injection
enforcement and makes the action policy return \emph{allow}; however, text-only
model input, privacy redaction, cancellation, and audit remain active. In
enforce mode, an action reaches the executor only after an immediate
\emph{allow} or after live-scope revalidation confirms an explicit approval or
matching run-local grant. Blocks, handoffs, timeouts, cancellations, missing
required context, and unresolved authority never execute $a_{t,i}$. A policy
handoff instead withholds the proposed browser action and lets the operator act
directly. Accordingly, the model retains planning and proposal power but cannot
independently authorise or directly execute privileged actions.

\section{Assets and trust boundary}

Sentinel protects more than data. Indeed, the operator's goal defines what the agent is
authorised to do, and the authenticated browser session, local files, and remote
application state determine what it is capable of changing. Private values,
consent decisions, and the audit record must also remain confidential.
Availability matters alongside these assets because an agent that never acts
provides no useful autonomy. Nevertheless, when Sentinel lacks context needed
for a security decision, refusing the effect is safer than guessing.

This leads to the main trust question: which component is allowed to turn a
proposal into a browser effect? A page may shape an observation, the model may
choose an action, and the extension may present a decision to the operator.
Influence, however, is not authority. None of those roles should be sufficient
to invoke the executor. \Cref{tab:trust-boundaries} makes that separation
explicit by recording what each component may contribute and what power remains
outside its role.

The allocation depends on Sentinel and the privileged executor retaining their
integrity. The boundary does not depend on the page, model, or extension making
correct security decisions. A hostile page may steer the
model towards a dangerous proposal, yet the proposal must still cross the
server-owned action guard. Similarly, a compromised extension may display
misleading state or withhold a response, but it cannot release a pending wait
or call the executor. The following requirements turn this allocation of power
into behaviour that can be tested.

\section{Adversary}

The primary adversary controls content that an agent encounters while pursuing
a legitimate task. This may be a page, user-generated record, link label,
extracted value, or stored tool result. The adversary knows the general system
design and attempts to replace the operator's goal, impersonate a higher-trust
role, suppress warnings, request disclosure, induce code or file access, or
persist influence into later turns. The action may be conspicuous, such as
cross-origin navigation, or use the same-origin form controls required by
the legitimate task.

The adversary controls content, not the components that enforce the boundary.
It does not compromise the operating system, browser binary, Sentinel process,
model provider, local policy file, or privileged executor. Their integrity is
an assumption of the claim.

\section{Security requirements}
\label{sec:security-objectives}

The requirements follow the life of one agent decision. First, observation
control limits what can shape the proposal. Next, action control decides what
that proposal may do in the current browser state. If operator input is needed,
authority control keeps the response narrow and non-transferable. Finally,
audit control records the outcome without reproducing the content that caused
the concern. Since these stages depend on one another, a weakness early in
the chain also weakens the assurances that follow.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{R\arabic*:},leftmargin=16mm]
  \item \textbf{Independent and complete action mediation.} The model's output
  is a proposal, not permission. Consequently, every privileged action entering
  the wrapped tool path is evaluated against live context immediately before
  execution, even when a similar action was allowed earlier. Each proposal also
  receives one attempted and one terminal audit outcome, including errors,
  denials, timeouts, handovers, and cancellation.

  \item \textbf{Verified observation handling.} A detector alert begins a
  security decision, it does not complete one. Browser, extraction, and
  tool-result content therefore remains untrusted regardless of origin. A
  detected region may continue only after attributable modification and clean
  re-detection of the reconstructed model input. If current-page content remains
  positive or cannot be attributed safely, Sentinel withholds it; bounded
  extraction or historical values are omitted in full.

  \item \textbf{Fail-closed policy and data flow.} Missing evidence cannot be
  treated as evidence that no rule applies. Domain blocks, unavailable required
  context, unsafe navigation schemes, and configured hard risk therefore take
  precedence over interactive approval. Likewise, recognised protected values
  cannot be typed, scripted, or uploaded by the agent in enforce mode; the
  operator performs that step directly.

  \item \textbf{Narrow authority.} An approval must identify the opportunity it
  authorises. It is therefore explicit, run-local, expiring, and bound to the
  reviewed action, origin, capabilities, taint, sensitive-data categories,
  target, and applicable parameter evidence. Before it can be used, Sentinel
  recomputes live policy and browser context. Silence, timeout, malformed
  responses, disconnect, and stop never become permission.

  \item \textbf{Privacy-preserving accountability.} The audit record must
  explain why an effect did or did not occur without becoming another route for
  disclosure. Decisions remain inspectable through redacted rules, risk
  factors, outcomes, and run-keyed digests. Persistent traces and extension
  telemetry therefore omit raw protected values, attacker payloads,
  screenshots, and unrestricted model reasoning by default.
\end{enumerate}

Taken together, R1--R5 describe one governed path. R2 controls the observation
that reaches the model; R1 and R3 mediate the resulting proposal; R4 constrains
any exceptional authority; and R5 preserves evidence of the outcome. The chain
does not make every permitted model decision correct, and a missed detection
can still produce a compromised proposal. For that reason, the evaluation pairs
contract tests of the individual controls with end-to-end security and utility
outcomes.

\section{Scope of the claim}

The claim is about an enforceable boundary, not an immune model. Sentinel does
not make every model judgement correct and does not claim to eliminate prompt
injection. It claims that, where observations and actions traverse the governed
BrowserUse path, \systemname\ independently evaluates the model's proposal.
Enforcement therefore does not depend on the model interpreting or obeying a
security warning. The empirical question is correspondingly narrow: under the
evaluated conditions, does that independent boundary reduce policy-violating
effects without preventing legitimate work?

Complete mediation ends at the governed BrowserUse path. Direct human page
interaction and browser effects invoked outside the wrapped tool path do not
cross Sentinel's guards. The service is also a local research prototype: origin
checks protect its loopback interface, but they do not isolate it from another
compromised process running as the same operating-system user. Finally, the
interactive model receives a DOM/accessibility representation. Pixel-only
instructions and tasks that require visual grounding are therefore outside the
measured channel. The attacks measured are those instantiated by AgentDojo and
WASP; adaptive attacks remain a limitation and a priority for the future
evaluation in \cref{chap:discussion}.

The evaluation must therefore establish security and usefulness together.
Detector recall is mechanism evidence; terminal attack failure can reflect an
incapable agent, just as blanket refusal can destroy utility. The thesis relies
on paired native security and task outcomes with trajectory records. Its
conclusions apply to the evaluated models, suites, representations, and frozen
implementations, not human comprehension or security effectiveness at
deployment scale.
