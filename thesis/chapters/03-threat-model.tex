\chapter{Threat model and security requirements}
\label{chap:threat-model}

\section{System model}

The operator supplies a trusted goal $G$. At step $t$, the browser produces
an observation $O_t$, the model proposes an action $a_t$, and the executor
may update environment state $S_t$:

\[
  O_t=\mathsf{observe}(S_t), \qquad
  a_t=\mathsf{agent}(G,O_{\leq t},A_{<t}), \qquad
  S_{t+1}=\mathsf{execute}(S_t,a_t).
\]

An ungoverned loop passes the model proposal directly to the executor.
\systemname\ inserts two transformations:

\[
  \widehat{O}_t=\mathsf{guard}_{O}(O_t,P,C_t), \qquad
  d_t=\mathsf{guard}_{A}(a_t,S_t,P,C_t),
\]

where $P$ is live policy and $C_t$ is run-local governance state. The
executor is called only when $d_t$ returns \emph{allow}, including an action
whose narrow approval remains valid after fresh preflight. Block, timeout,
cancellation, unavailable required context, and unresolved authority do not
execute $a_t$. The model is therefore a proposal generator rather than the
source of authority for privileged effects.

\section{Assets and trust boundary}

Protected assets are the operator's task authority, authenticated browser
session, private and security-sensitive data, local files available to the
browser, integrity of remote state, consent decisions, and audit
confidentiality. Availability is also relevant because an agent that never
acts has no useful autonomy, although denial is preferable to an unauthorised
effect when required security context is missing.

The threat model turns on one question: which component can cause a browser
effect to occur? A page can influence an observation, a model can propose an
action, and an extension can display a decision, but none of those roles should
confer permission to execute. \Cref{tab:trust-boundaries} is therefore an
authority ledger. It states the permitted role of each component and, more
importantly, the power that remains outside that role.

The table makes the central assumption visible. Sentinel and the privileged
executor must retain their integrity; the model, page, or extension need not be
trusted to make correct security decisions. A compromised extension may show
misleading state or withhold a response, but it cannot release the server's
wait. A compromised page may shape what the model proposes, but it still cannot
call the executor. The requirements below turn this allocation into behaviour
that can be tested.

\section{Adversary}

The primary adversary controls content that an agent encounters while pursuing
a legitimate task. This may be a page, user-generated record, link label,
extracted value, or stored tool result. The adversary knows the general system
design and attempts to replace the operator's goal, impersonate a higher-trust
role, suppress warnings, request disclosure, induce code or file access, or
persist influence into later turns. The action may be conspicuous, such as
cross-origin navigation, or use the same same-origin form controls required by
the legitimate task.

The adversary does not compromise the operating system, browser binary,
Sentinel process, model provider, or local policy file. Direct human page
interaction and browser effects outside the governed BrowserUse tool path are
also outside complete mediation. The evaluated attacks are those instantiated
by AgentDojo and WASP. Adaptive and pixel-only attacks are outside the measured
task matrices; they remain relevant limitations and motivate the future
evaluations identified in \cref{chap:discussion}.

\section{Security requirements}
\label{sec:security-objectives}

The requirements form a dependency chain rather than a list of desirable
features. Observation control limits what can shape a proposal; action control
limits what that proposal can do; authority control prevents an interface
response from becoming a transferable permission; audit control makes every
outcome accountable without replaying the attack. A break in any earlier link
weakens the guarantees that follow.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{R\arabic*:},leftmargin=16mm]
  \item \textbf{Independent and complete action mediation.} No proposal is
  trusted because the model produced it or because a similar action was
  previously allowed. Every privileged action entering the wrapped tool path is
  evaluated against live context immediately before execution. Every attempt
  receives one attempted and one terminal audit outcome, including errors,
  denials, timeouts, handovers, and cancellation.

  \item \textbf{Verified observation handling.} Detection is not itself a
  safety property. Browser, extraction, and tool-result content remains
  untrusted regardless of origin, and a detected region may be forwarded only
  after attributable modification and clean re-detection of the reconstructed
  model input. Known residual or unattributable current-page content is
  withheld; bounded extraction or historical values are omitted in full.

  \item \textbf{Fail-closed policy and data flow.} Uncertainty cannot be
  interpreted as an absent rule. Domain blocks, unavailable required context,
  unsafe navigation schemes, and configured hard risk take precedence over
  interactive approval. Recognised protected values cannot be typed, scripted,
  or uploaded by the agent in enforce mode; the human performs the step
  directly.

  \item \textbf{Narrow authority.} Consent is a scoped capability, not a
  conversational sentiment. Approval is explicit, run-local, expiring, and
  bound to the reviewed action, origin, capabilities, taint, sensitive-data
  categories, target, and applicable parameter evidence. Live policy and
  browser context are recomputed before use. Silence, timeout, malformed
  responses, disconnect, and stop never become permission.

  \item \textbf{Privacy-preserving accountability.} An audit trail must explain
  why an effect did or did not occur without becoming a second disclosure
  channel. Decisions remain inspectable through redacted rules, risk factors,
  outcomes, and run-keyed digests. Raw protected values, attacker payloads,
  visual captures, and unrestricted model rationale are excluded from
  persistent and extension telemetry by default.
\end{enumerate}

Together, R1--R5 define a compact safety case for the paths Sentinel controls:
the model sees only an observation that passed R2, its proposal reaches the
executor only through R1 and R3, any exceptional permission must satisfy R4,
and R5 preserves evidence of the outcome. They do not make every allowed model
decision correct, and a missed detection can still produce a compromised
proposal. The evaluation therefore combines contract tests with end-to-end
security and utility outcomes.

\section{Scope of the claim}

The claim is about an enforceable boundary, not an immune model. Sentinel does
not make every model judgement correct and does not claim to eliminate prompt
injection. It claims that, where observations and actions traverse the governed
BrowserUse path, the model's proposal can be subjected to a decision whose
authority does not depend on the model obeying a warning. The empirical question
is correspondingly narrow: under the evaluated conditions, does that independent
boundary reduce policy-violating effects without preventing legitimate work?

That claim has a visible edge. The interactive model receives a
DOM/accessibility representation, so pixel-only instructions and tasks that
require visual grounding are not measured. The service is a local research
prototype: origin checks protect its loopback interface, but another compromised
process running as the same operating-system user is outside the isolation
assumption. Direct human interaction, actions taken outside the wrapped tool
path, and compromise of Sentinel, the browser, or the operating system are also
outside complete mediation.

The evaluation must therefore establish security and usefulness together.
Detector recall is mechanism evidence, not the endpoint; terminal attack failure
can reflect an incapable agent, just as blanket refusal can create the appearance
of safety by destroying utility. The thesis consequently relies on paired native
security and task outcomes, supplemented by trajectory and intervention records.
Its conclusions apply to those models, suites, representations, and frozen
implementations, and do not constitute evidence about human comprehension or
deployment-scale robustness.
