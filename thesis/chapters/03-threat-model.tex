\chapter{Threat model and security requirements}
\label{chap:threat-model}

\section{System model}

The operator begins by supplying a trusted goal $G$. At planning step $t$, the
browser-agent runtime samples browser state $S_t^O$ and forms an ungoverned
observation $O_t$. Once the model input crosses the observation boundary, the
model may propose a finite sequence
$A_t=(a_{t,1},\ldots,a_{t,n_t})$. BrowserUse then attempts an ordered prefix of
that sequence; let $m_t\leq n_t$ denote the number attempted. Each attempted
action is mediated against live state immediately before execution, and
$d_{t,i}$ records the resulting refusal, handoff outcome, or tool result. The
subscript $<t$ denotes history before planning step $t$:

\[
\begin{aligned}
  O_t &= \mathsf{observe}(S_t^O), &
  M_t &= \mathsf{compose}(G,O_t,H_{<t}), \\
  \widehat{M}_t &= \mathsf{guard}_{M}(M_t,P_t^O,C_t), &
  A_t &= \mathsf{agent}(\widehat{M}_t), \\
  d_{t,i} &=
  \mathsf{govern}_{A}(a_{t,i},S_{t,i}^A,P_{t,i}^A,C_{t,i}),
  \quad 1\leq i\leq m_t.
\end{aligned}
\]

Here, $D_t=(d_{t,1},\ldots,d_{t,m_t})$ collects attempted-action outcomes, while
$H_{<t}$ denotes BrowserUse's retained or compacted step history. That history
contains prior model summaries and action-outcome text, not complete raw
observations or exact earlier proposals. The message $M_t$ therefore combines
the trusted goal, current observation, and retained history; $\widehat{M}_t$ is
the governed form supplied to the model. Consequently, untrusted observations
and tool-result text cross the same model-input boundary.

The state symbols show when browser context is read. $S_t^O$ is sampled to
construct the observation, whereas each $S_{t,i}^A$ is sampled immediately
before its action, allowing the page or browser to evolve between planning and
execution. Similarly, $P_t^O$ and $P_{t,i}^A$ are live policy snapshots at the
model and action boundaries, while $C_t$ and $C_{t,i}$ are run-local governance
states. BrowserUse abandons any unattempted suffix after an error, a
sequence-terminating action, or a URL or focus change.

At these complementary boundaries, the model-input guard may sanitise or omit
untrusted regions. It withholds an unresolved current observation until fresh
recapture or fail-closed termination, while the action governor wraps the
executor itself, not merely the policy decision. Off mode disables prompt-injection
enforcement and makes the action policy return \emph{allow}; however, text-only
model input, privacy redaction, cancellation, and audit remain active. In
enforce mode, an action reaches the executor only after an immediate
\emph{allow} or after live-scope revalidation confirms an explicit approval or
matching run-local grant. Blocks, handoffs, timeouts, cancellations, missing
required context, and unresolved authority never execute $a_{t,i}$. A policy
handoff instead withholds the proposed browser action and lets the operator act
directly. Accordingly, the model retains planning and proposal power but cannot
independently authorise or directly execute privileged actions.

\section{Assets and trust boundary}

Sentinel protects both information and authority. The operator's goal defines
what the agent is authorised to do, while the authenticated browser session,
local files, and remote application state determine what it is capable of
changing. Private values, consent decisions, and the audit record must also
remain confidential. At the same time, availability matters because an agent
that never acts provides no useful autonomy. This need for usefulness does not,
however, justify guessing: when Sentinel lacks the context required for a
security decision, it refuses the effect.

The central trust question is therefore not which components may influence a
decision, but which component may turn a proposal into a browser effect. A page
may shape an observation, the model may choose an action, and the extension may
present a decision to the operator. Yet influence is not authority: none of
these roles alone is sufficient to invoke the executor.

Given these integrity and relay assumptions, the boundary does not depend on
the page or model behaving securely. A hostile page may steer the model towards
a dangerous proposal, but the server-owned action guard still mediates that
proposal before execution. A compromised extension could answer an active
request or alter policy, but it could not call the executor, reuse stale
authority, or bypass revalidation. The security requirements below formalise
the controls needed to preserve these limits.

\section{Adversary}

The primary adversary controls content that the agent encounters while pursuing
a legitimate task. This influence may originate in a page, user-generated
record, link label, extracted value, or stored tool result. The adversary knows
the general system design and seeks to replace the operator's goal,
impersonate a higher-trust role, suppress warnings, request disclosure, induce
code or file access, or preserve malicious influence across later turns. The
resulting action may be conspicuous, such as cross-origin navigation, or it may
use the same-origin form controls required by the legitimate task.

Under this threat model, the adversary controls content but not the components
that enforce the boundary. It does not compromise the operating system, browser
binary, Sentinel process, model provider, local policy file, or privileged
executor. The claim therefore assumes the integrity of those components.
Sentinel does not establish it.

\section{Security requirements}
\label{sec:security-objectives}

The requirements follow the life cycle of a single agent decision. First,
observation control limits what can shape the proposal. Action control then
decides what that proposal may do in the current browser state. If operator
input becomes necessary, authority control keeps the response narrow and
non-transferable. Finally, audit control records the outcome without
reproducing the content that caused concern. Since these stages depend on one
another, a weakness early in the chain also weakens the assurances that
follow.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{R\arabic*:},leftmargin=16mm]
  \item \textbf{Independent and complete action mediation.} The model's output
  is a proposal, not permission. Consequently, every privileged action entering
  the wrapped tool path is evaluated against live context immediately before
  execution, even if a similar action was allowed earlier. Each attempted
  action also receives exactly one attempted and one terminal audit outcome,
  including errors, denials, timeouts, handovers, and cancellation.

  \item \textbf{Verified observation handling.} A detector alert begins a
  security decision; it does not complete one. Browser, extraction, and
  tool-result content therefore remains untrusted regardless of origin. A
  detected region may continue only after attributable modification and clean
  re-detection of the reconstructed model input. If current-page content still
  tests positive or cannot be attributed safely, Sentinel withholds it;
  bounded extraction or historical values are instead omitted in full.

  \item \textbf{Fail-closed policy and data flow.} Missing evidence cannot be
  treated as evidence that no rule applies. Domain blocks, unavailable required
  context, unsafe navigation schemes, and configured hard risk therefore take
  precedence over interactive approval. For the same reason, recognised
  protected values cannot be typed, scripted, or uploaded by the agent in
  enforce mode; the operator must perform that step directly.

  \item \textbf{Narrow authority.} An approval must identify precisely what it
  authorises. It is therefore explicit, run-local, expiring, and bound to the
  reviewed action, origin, capabilities, taint, sensitive-data categories,
  target, and applicable parameter evidence. Before using that authority,
  Sentinel recomputes live policy and browser context. Silence, timeout,
  malformed responses, disconnect, and stop never become permission.

  \item \textbf{Privacy-preserving accountability.} The audit record must
  explain why an effect did or did not occur without creating another route for
  disclosure. Decisions remain inspectable through redacted rules, risk
  factors, outcomes, and run-keyed digests. Consequently, persistent traces and
  extension telemetry omit raw protected values, attacker payloads,
  screenshots, and unrestricted model reasoning by default.
\end{enumerate}

Together, R1--R5 define a single governed path. R2 controls the observation
that reaches the model; R1 and R3 mediate the resulting proposal; R4 constrains
any exceptional authority; and R5 preserves evidence of the outcome. This
chain does not make every permitted model decision correct, and a missed
detection can still produce a compromised proposal. The evaluation therefore
pairs contract tests of the individual controls with end-to-end security and
utility outcomes.

\section{Scope of the claim}

The claim concerns an enforceable boundary, not an immune model. Sentinel does
not make every model judgement correct, nor does it claim to eliminate prompt
injection. Instead, where observations and actions traverse the governed
BrowserUse path, \systemname\ independently evaluates the model's proposal.
Enforcement therefore does not depend on the model correctly interpreting or
obeying a security warning. The empirical question is correspondingly narrow:
under the evaluated conditions, does this independent boundary reduce
policy-violating effects without preventing legitimate work?

However, complete mediation ends at the governed BrowserUse path. Direct human
page interaction and browser effects invoked outside the wrapped tool path do
not cross Sentinel's guards. Moreover, the service is a local research
prototype: origin checks protect its loopback interface, but they do not
isolate it from another compromised process running as the same
operating-system user. The interactive model also receives only a
DOM/accessibility representation. Pixel-only instructions and tasks that
require visual grounding therefore fall outside the measured channel. Finally,
the measured attacks are those instantiated by AgentDojo and WASP; adaptive
attacks remain a limitation and a priority for the future evaluation discussed
in \cref{chap:discussion}.

The evaluation must therefore establish security and usefulness together.
Detector recall provides mechanism evidence, but terminal attack failure may
also reflect an incapable agent, just as blanket refusal may destroy utility.
For this reason, the thesis relies on paired native security and task outcomes,
supported by trajectory records. Its conclusions apply to the evaluated
models, suites, representations, and frozen implementations, not human
comprehension or security effectiveness at deployment scale.
