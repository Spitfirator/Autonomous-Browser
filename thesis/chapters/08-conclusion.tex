\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

Browser agents must interpret web content to act, yet that content can also
redirect them. This dissertation asked whether independent runtime enforcement
can reduce such violations while preserving useful task completion.
\systemname\ separates planning from authority: BrowserUse and the model choose
actions, while observation handling and privileged execution are governed
outside the model.

Sentinel contributes an executor-side architecture that mediates live action
context through one policy, fail-closed outcomes, and paired audit; a
post-verified observation boundary coupled to narrow approval, direct human
steps, and fresh-capture incidents; and a two-benchmark evaluation connecting
controlled AgentDojo mechanism evidence to replicated browser-native WASP
evidence. A Chrome extension presents the server-owned authority states and
their redacted history.

The empirical answer is positive within that boundary. Across the three
matched AgentDojo comparisons, attacker success fell from 20.8--32.8\% with
enforcement off to 0--0.2\% with enforcement on, while utility increased for
every model. In the three-seed 27B WASP experiment, full enforcement changed
action-audited terminal success from 17/252 to 0/252 and attacker-directed
execution from 42/252 to 0/252. Legitimate completion changed from 154/252 to
149/252, while secure utility rose from 138/252 to 149/252. Moreover, the
policy-only/full contrast isolated sanitisation's additional contribution: the
15 episodes with attacker-directed execution were absent under full enforcement
at the same aggregate utility.

These findings remain bounded to the tested text-only channels, models, tasks,
and prototype isolation. They do not establish universal detection,
deployment-scale isolation, or human effectiveness. Accordingly, the next
steps are held-out adaptive evaluation, visual mediation, a tested
task-authority contract, and a human-in-the-loop study of the implemented
authority workflow.

The broader conclusion is architectural: under the tested conditions, useful
planning did not require unchecked authority. Sentinel governed what reached
the planner and what left it while preserving useful autonomy.
