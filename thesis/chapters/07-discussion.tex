\chapter{Discussion}
\label{chap:discussion}

\section{Answer to the research question}

Within the evaluated boundary, independent runtime enforcement reduced
prompt-injection-driven violations while preserving useful task completion.
This conclusion rests on the conjunction of three observations.

First, native AgentDojo attacker success fell from 206, 131, and 203 cases to
0, 1, and 0 for the 4B, 9B, and 27B conditions. Secure utility increased by
149, 100, and 138 cases. Each model recorded only nine explicit action blocks,
so the result was not produced by preventing the agent from acting.

Second, the replicated WASP comparison transferred the mechanism to a different
agent scaffold and an actual browser. Across three seeds, full enforcement
changed attacker-directed execution from 42/252 episodes to zero and
action-audited terminal success from 17/252 to zero. Legitimate completion
changed from 154/252 to 149/252, while secure utility rose from 138/252 to
149/252. The security gain therefore coexisted with a small aggregate utility
cost and substantial between-seed variation, both of which remain visible.

Third, the sanitisation ablation and telemetry provide a consistent mechanism
account. Enforcement without sanitisation still left attacker-directed
execution in 15/252 episodes. The full condition recorded 740 confirmed
removals, no residual or unattributable observation, and no attacker-directed
proposal. AgentDojo likewise recorded sanitised tool output in most attacked
enforce cases and few explicit blocks. Across both environments, the recurring
intervention was removal of hostile observation content before the next model
decision, backed by policy at execution time.

These findings support the architectural thesis. A probabilistic planner can
remain responsible for choosing useful actions without also being the sole
authority for whether those actions execute. The observation guard limits one
source of hostile influence; the action guard supplies a stable decision point
when influence or ordinary model error remains.

\section{Security and utility are coupled}

The AgentDojo results show that security and utility did not move in opposite
directions: utility increased under enforcement for all three models. Indirect
injection explains how this can happen. An attack can consume steps, alter
memory, and redirect the agent away from the user's task. Removing the injected
result can therefore recover task completion as well as suppress the attacker
goal.

This observation changes how a defence should be evaluated. Clean utility
estimates model capability without an attack; it is not the counterfactual for
an attacked trajectory. The relevant effect compares the same task and attack
with and without enforcement. Secure utility captures the joint outcome, while
paired gains and losses show whether an aggregate improvement conceals
individual regressions.

The difference between observation sanitisation and blanket action refusal is
also important. A hard error on every tainted interaction can force repeated
replanning and exhaust the action or approval budget. Removing the hostile
source lets the planner continue through ordinary same-origin controls. The
AgentDojo gains and small aggregate WASP utility difference suggest that
earlier, attributable intervention can be less disruptive than repeatedly
vetoing effects after a trajectory has already diverged.

\section{Why the two enforcement points are complementary}

The results distinguish the purpose of the observation and action boundaries.
The observation guard acts before the planner commits to a trajectory. In the
final WASP condition it removed every detected hostile region before inference,
and none of the attacker-directed executions seen in 42 off episodes or 15
policy-only episodes was proposed. This is more than a presentation filter: it
changes the information on which the model's next plan is conditioned. The
policy-only condition preserves the remaining enforcement boundary while
withholding that rewrite, so the contrast directly isolates the configured
sanitisation increment. The earlier 9B warning result supplies consistent
failure analysis: exposing both the attack and a warning to the same planner
did not improve either terminal or intermediate attack outcomes.

The action guard addresses a different residual problem. No detector is
complete, sanitisation may be unavailable for an effect proposed from earlier
state, and an unprompted model error can reach the same privileged tool. A
policy decision immediately before execution can enforce stable invariants
without inferring the full intention behind every action. AgentDojo recorded
nine sensitive-data-flow blocks per model after hundreds of observations had
been sanitised. Those blocks were numerically secondary, but they show that
pre-model rewriting and pre-tool control are not interchangeable.

Using only the action boundary would also change the utility profile. The
system would allow the hostile observation to consume context and planning
steps, then attempt to recognise the resulting effects one by one. Because
ordinary and attacker-directed work may share tools and origins, a broad policy
would have to refuse legitimate effects, whereas a narrow policy would miss
semantically disguised ones. Using only sanitisation would avoid that trade-off
for detected text but leave no independent authority for protected data, unsafe
schemes, uploads, or consequential actions. The two-boundary design therefore
allocates each problem to the point at which the relevant fact is observable:
content provenance before planning and concrete capability, target, and live
state before execution.

This composition also explains why Sentinel can remain model-independent. The
planner need not expose hidden reasoning or implement a special security API.
It receives a governed message and returns the same tool proposal expected by
BrowserUse. The enforcement layer can then evaluate deterministic context that
would be awkward or unsafe to place in the prompt, such as protected-value
classes and local file inspection. Model independence here means interface
compatibility, not equal performance: each model still determines how well the
remaining task is planned.

\section{What the sanitisation ablation establishes}

The final ablation sharpens the mechanism claim. Policy-only combines boundary
neutralisation, a detector warning, and action policy; relative to off it
reduced terminal attack success from 17/252 to 1/252 and attacker-directed
execution from 42/252 to 15/252. That contrast is useful but composite. It
cannot identify which of its three active mechanisms caused the change.

Full differs only by enabling browser sanitisation. The remaining 15
attacker-executing episodes fell to zero, with a five-episode decrement in every
seed; aggregate utility remained 149/252. Terminal success changed by only one
episode because most policy-only diversions already failed to complete the
attacker goal. The action audit is therefore essential: it shows that verified
removal changed what the model attempted, not only the last step of a mostly
incapable baseline.

The historical 9B runs also explain why the final ablation was necessary.
Warning-only enforcement left ASR unchanged and increased attacker-directed
execution. Because provenance changed during development, these runs are not
pooled causal estimates. They nevertheless motivated a clear post-condition:
the exact reconstructed model input must change and then pass re-detection.

That post-condition also motivates current unresolved-content behaviour. If
attribution or clean re-detection fails, sending the suspicious content with a
warning recreates the ineffective authority structure. Sentinel instead
withholds the current observation and permits continuation only from a fresh
capture after operator review. The final WASP rows recorded no such unresolved
case; tests and an implementation walkthrough establish the incident mechanism,
but the benchmark does not measure its effect.

\section{Intermediate execution and security by incompetence}

WASP describes a form of ``security by incompetence'': an agent can begin
serving an attacker yet fail to complete the terminal objective
~\cite{evtimov2025wasp}. The replicated off condition makes the gap measurable.
Seventeen episodes satisfied the final attacker oracle, while 42 executed at
least one attacker-directed action. In 25 episodes, an attacker action reached
the browser but the terminal goal still failed. That category directly
demonstrates inability after execution; the 209 episodes with no recorded
attempt are more ambiguous and are not credited as incapability.

Terminal ASR remains necessary because it uses the benchmark's environmental
ground truth, but it is insufficient by itself. Intermediate attempt and
execution expose unwanted navigation or side effects that fail only because
the agent is incapable of finishing. They also distinguish model proposals
from actions that Sentinel actually allowed to reach the tool. The replay is
conservative: it matches the instantiated attacker goals and cannot classify
reasoning-only diversion or an unforeseen semantically equivalent action.

The same principle constrains cross-model interpretation. Off-condition ASR is
not monotonic from 4B to 27B, and the models differ in capability as well as
size. A more capable agent may complete more legitimate and malicious goals.
Security comparisons should therefore report utility, attacker outcome, and
executed effects together. Neither model size nor a low ASR is an independent
security axis.

\section{Policy as a backstop}

Most WASP attacker goals culminate in the same-origin forms and controls
used by legitimate tasks. Treating every side effect after a detector finding
as approval-requiring would convert untrusted provenance into broad denial of
service. Sentinel applies strong deterministic rules to
independently dangerous sinks, including cross-origin flow, protected values,
local files, code, unsafe schemes, and configured consequential targets, while
post-verified sanitisation handles the observation that shaped the plan.

This division is pragmatic, but it is not a complete semantic judge. A
natural-looking same-origin instruction can evade the detector and resemble a
legitimate task step. Task Shield, MELON, and DRIFT introduce task-derived or
counterfactual abstractions that can address semantic alignment
~\cite{jia2025taskshield,zhu2025melon,li2025drift}. A promising composition is
to retain deterministic executor policy for non-negotiable invariants while a
separately isolated component checks whether ambiguous effects remain
consistent with a trusted task representation.

Selective authority supports the same division. Hard refusal is used where
policy already resolves the question. Narrow approval is reserved for a
permissible but consequential proposal. Direct takeover changes who performs a
protected step, and an observation incident prevents hostile input from
reaching the planner. Treating these as different protocol states avoids the
common failure in which one generic confirmation is asked to serve as both
policy exception and secret-entry mechanism.

The distinction is also temporal. An approval card represents a proposal that
may become stale while the operator reads it, so authority is useful only after
the server revalidates the live target and parameters. A takeover represents a
period during which the operator owns the page and the intercepted agent action
must remain suppressed. An incident represents an observation that was never
safe to send to the model, so there is no action to approve at all. These states
have different completion conditions even if the interface renders each as an
interruption.

The contribution here is a completed systems mechanism; no second behavioural
study is claimed. The implementation supplies expiring scopes,
one-terminal-outcome audit, timeout and cancellation, fresh-observation
semantics, and browser-integrated presentation. These properties make the
authority path reproducible and testable. Furthermore, an unavailable interface
cannot silently become permission. Whether people interpret the cards correctly
remains a separate empirical question; it is not needed to establish that the
acting model does not own the decision.

\section{Implications for system-level defences}

The architectural comparison in \cref{tab:system-comparison} located Sentinel
among systems that move security decisions outside the acting model. The results
now clarify what its particular trade-off delivers. Sentinel does not inherit
the formal guarantees of CaMeL or Fides because it keeps an existing free-form
browser loop. Instead, it supplies empirical evidence that exact pre-model
rewriting, immediate pre-tool enforcement, server-owned authority, and paired
audit can operate together while retaining useful task performance. Structured
planners offer stronger guarantees for tasks they can represent; an
executor-side wrapper offers a practical boundary when the agent exposes no
trusted program.

This distinction also identifies a useful hybrid. A structured task abstraction
could judge whether ambiguous content and effects remain relevant to the user's
goal, while Sentinel's executor boundary continues to enforce non-negotiable
local rules and bind human authority to live browser state. Such a design would
reduce reliance on surface signatures without returning the final decision to
the acting model. Its value would depend on whether the semantic monitor adds
coverage without enough false intervention to erase the utility retained here.

\section{Limitations and validity}
\label{sec:discussion-limitations}

Iterative fixes are a strength of the engineering process: observed failures
revealed gaps and led to a better enforcement boundary. The validity limitation
comes from evaluating detector coverage on attack families that also informed
those fixes. For example, Sentinel's signatures were revised after AgentDojo
and WASP development failures, including a correction for URL attacks whose
hidden link target was absent from BrowserUse's accessibility text. The
scorecard should therefore be read as regression coverage for known families,
not as an estimate of performance on unseen attacks or other benchmarks. Clean
re-detection establishes a configuration-specific post-condition; it cannot
show that the remaining text is free of attacker intent. Adaptive optimisation
against a frozen whole system is the appropriate next test
~\cite{zhan2025adaptive}.

The results do not extend to visual observations or tasks that require visual
grounding. A future visual path needs its own provenance, isolation, and
action-authority design because adding images expands both capability and attack
surface~\cite{bailey2024imagehijacks,koh2024visualwebarena}.

Experimental scope is bounded by three Qwen AgentDojo rows, three WASP sampling
seeds over one fixed 84-case matrix, one pinned WASP revision, a 30-step browser
budget, and the configured policies. Website state was reset before every cell.
However, the controller always ran the cells in the same sequence, so a change
in runtime conditions over time could align with a particular condition.
Randomising or counterbalancing the sequence would reduce that risk. The 9B
WASP rows are development revisions and are not part of the final controlled
experiment. Some
historical AgentDojo artefacts also predate the final provenance schema, which
limits exact environment reconstruction even though task identities and native
counts are preserved. These constraints preclude deployment-population and
model-scaling claims.

The browser and policy boundaries remain finite. Pattern-based protected-data
classification can miss a semantically sensitive value, and an allowed
same-origin click can have server-side consequences unavailable to local DOM
inspection. Loopback origin checks are suitable for the local prototype but do
not isolate it from a compromised same-user process. Current hardening of
incidents, secret takeover, revalidation, extraction/history handling, and
special URLs is supported by tests; the frozen benchmark rows did not exercise
those mechanisms.

\subsection{Ethics and responsible research}

No human participated in the reported studies, and no participant data was
collected. Scripted benchmark modes supplied reproducible control behaviour;
approval counts, pause duration, and Activity annotations are therefore
technical telemetry. Likewise, the interface walkthroughs establish
server-owned request semantics and rendered states, but they do not measure
comprehension, trust, workload, habituation, or recovery quality.

The browser evidence was produced in controlled benchmark environments and
with local synthetic interface fixtures. The submitted figures contain no live
credential, private account, private URL, or raw attack payload, and the
dissertation does not reproduce the benchmark's operational injection text.
These choices reduce unnecessary disclosure, although the work remains dual
use because the same analysis can inform attacks as well as defences. Any
future study of real operator decisions would therefore require ethics approval
before recruitment or data collection, together with a protocol for consent,
data minimisation, and secure handling of interaction records.

\section{Implications and future work}

The immediate research priority is a frozen adaptive evaluation: fix detector,
policy, and middleware, then test unseen prompt families and optimisation
targeted at the combined observation/action boundary on held-out tasks and a
larger randomised seed design.
The next architectural step is a short, operator-visible task contract that
states expected origins, data classes, permitted effects, human steps, and stop
conditions before exposure to untrusted content. It should be evaluated as a
separate mechanism in future work; the present rows cannot supply that evidence
retrospectively.

Visual mediation and human evaluation form distinct work packages. A visual
guard should emit bounded, provenance-aware representations and connect
uncertain pixel-derived actions to stricter authority. Human evaluation should
begin with expert cognitive walkthroughs, then measure whether approval,
takeover, and incident review lead to correct decisions at an acceptable
attention cost. In parallel, deployment hardening should authenticate local
control, separate process privileges, protect policy and trace keys, and
consider transactional browser environments.

The broader implication is that useful agent autonomy and independent
authority are compatible design goals. Sentinel's results do not require the
planner to become a perfect security classifier; they show that the system can
change what reaches the planner, constrain what leaves it, and preserve enough
freedom for useful work.
