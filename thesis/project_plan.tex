\section{Working title and thesis}

\textbf{Working title:} \emph{Design and Implementation of Enforcement
Middleware for Securing Web Agents}.

\textbf{Thesis:} Browser agents should not be relied upon to police the same
untrusted content that determines their actions.  A separate, executor-side
runtime can instead mediate observations, data flow, and privileged actions.
The dissertation tests the bounded claim that this architecture can reduce
evaluated policy violations while preserving useful autonomy under the tested
conditions.  It does not claim to solve prompt injection or establish
deployment readiness.

\section{Research questions}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*:},leftmargin=18mm]
  \item Can a separate enforcement layer reduce unsafe browser-agent actions
  without stopping the agent from doing useful work?
  \item Under what conditions should an independent runtime withhold a
  browser-agent action or observation, and what fail-closed protocol should
  govern approval, takeover, and return of control?
\end{enumerate}

RQ1 is answered empirically using AgentDojo and WASP. RQ2 receives a bounded
design and protocol-verification answer through selected implementation
contracts and rendered client states. Full browser reconnect, accessibility,
schema equivalence, and human comprehension, workload, trust, and approval
behaviour are not evaluated.

\section{Original contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item An executor-side reference-monitor architecture that treats the model
  and every browser-derived observation as untrusted and applies policy
  immediately before privileged execution.
  \item A layered observation boundary combining deterministic detection,
  attributable sanitisation, clean re-detection, sticky origin-scoped taint,
  and fail-closed handling of unresolved content.
  \item A selective-authority mechanism for hard blocks, bounded approval,
  direct human takeover, cancellation, revalidation, and privacy-preserving
  audit.
  \item A two-stage evaluation that uses AgentDojo for controlled mechanism
  evidence and WASP for browser-native transfer and failure analysis, reporting
  utility and secure utility alongside attack success and attacker-directed
  execution.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Evidence boundaries that must remain explicit}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Use the matched Qwen 3.5 27B WASP row as the principal browser-native
  comparison.  Use the 9B sequence only as provenance-separated development
  and failure-analysis evidence.
  \item Report native user utility, secure utility, native attack success,
  attacker-directed attempts and executions, interventions, and provenance
  together.  Low attack success alone is not security evidence.
  \item Treat the detector corpora as development evidence where signatures
  were adapted after inspection.  Do not convert scorecard performance into
  an end-to-end claim.
  \item Distinguish frozen campaign behaviour from later implementation:
  unresolved-observation quarantine, bounded extraction/tool-result removal,
  human-only secret takeover, approval revalidation, lifecycle hardening,
  screenshot exclusion in the ordinary runner, and the special-URL boundary
  post-date the benchmark rows.
  \item Do not describe scripted benchmark participant modes as humans.  No
  WASP oracle campaign or participant study exists.
  \item Activity's security-pause timing and per-decision
  \texttt{understood} ticks are technical instrumentation and walkthrough
  annotations, not behavioural evidence.
\end{itemize}

\section{Submission-quality gates}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every external factual claim has a verified primary or peer-reviewed
  source in BibTeX; no placeholder or guessed citation records remain.
  \item Every numerical result is checked against a curated JSON/Markdown
  result artifact and its frozen provenance.
  \item Terminology, denominators, policy modes, and measured/post-campaign
  boundaries are consistent across chapters.
  \item The official root compiles with Tectonic without undefined
  references, missing citations, overfull boxes, or placeholder text.
  \item The compiled PDF is at most 50 main-text pages and 100 pages in total,
  and every page is rendered and visually inspected for layout defects.
  \item Title-page identity, degree, supervisor, submission date, declaration,
  and distribution wording are confirmed by the author before submission.
\end{enumerate}
