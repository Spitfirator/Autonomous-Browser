\section{Working title and thesis}

\textbf{Title:} \emph{Sentinel: Independent Runtime Enforcement for Browser
Agents}.

\textbf{Thesis:} Browser agents should not be relied upon to police the same
untrusted content that determines their actions.  A separate, executor-side
runtime can instead mediate observations, data flow, and privileged actions.
The dissertation tests the bounded claim that this architecture can reduce
evaluated policy violations while preserving useful autonomy under the tested
conditions.  It does not claim to solve prompt injection or establish
deployment readiness.

\section{Research question}

\begin{quote}
\textbf{RQ.} To what extent can an independent runtime-enforcement layer reduce
prompt-injection-driven browser-agent violations while preserving task utility?
\end{quote}

AgentDojo and WASP answer this question empirically at controlled tool and
browser-native levels. Approval, direct takeover, unresolved-observation
incidents, audit, and the Chrome extension are completed supporting mechanisms
within the bounded prototype. They are not a second empirical question or
evidence about human effectiveness.

\section{Original contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Executor-side mediation of model-proposed privileged actions.
  \item Post-verified observation sanitisation combined with selective,
  fail-closed human authority.
  \item Evaluation across controlled AgentDojo environments and browser-native
  WASP tasks, reporting utility and secure utility alongside native attacker
  success and attacker-directed execution.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Evidence boundaries that must remain explicit}

\begin{itemize}
  \item Use the matched Qwen 3.5 27B WASP row as the principal browser-native
  comparison.  Use the 9B sequence only as provenance-separated development
  and failure-analysis evidence.
  \item Report native user utility, secure utility, native attack success,
  attacker-directed attempts and executions, interventions, and provenance
  together.  Low attack success alone is not security evidence.
  \item Treat the detector corpora as development evidence where signatures
  were adapted after inspection.  Do not convert scorecard performance into
  an end-to-end claim.
  \item Distinguish frozen campaign behaviour from later implementation:
  unresolved-observation quarantine, bounded extraction/tool-result removal,
  human-only secret takeover, approval revalidation, lifecycle hardening,
  screenshot exclusion in the ordinary runner, and the special-URL boundary
  post-date the benchmark rows.
  \item Do not describe scripted benchmark participant modes as humans.  No
  WASP oracle campaign or participant study exists.
  \item Activity's security-pause timing and per-decision
  \texttt{understood} ticks are technical instrumentation and walkthrough
  annotations, not behavioural evidence.
\end{itemize}
