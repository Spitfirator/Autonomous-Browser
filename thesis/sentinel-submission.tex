\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
\begin{singlespace}

Browser agents turn model outputs into consequential actions, yet the pages
they must interpret may also contain instructions supplied by an attacker.
This creates an authority problem: the model must interpret untrusted content
while deciding whether that content should influence privileged effects. This
dissertation presents \systemname, an independent enforcement layer between a
browser agent and its executor. \systemname\ governs observations before they
reach the model and evaluates proposed actions immediately before execution.
When policy requires operator authority, it can request narrow approval or
pause for a direct human step. It combines post-verified observation
sanitisation, origin-scoped taint, deterministic policy and data-flow rules,
protected-input handling, and redacted audit. A Chrome extension provides the
browser-facing control plane: it displays redacted runtime decisions and
relays scoped operator responses, while enforcement authority remains in the
runtime.

The evaluation links controlled AgentDojo evidence with replicated
browser-native WASP results. Across three matched AgentDojo comparisons, each
with 629 attacked tasks per condition, baseline attacker success was 32.8\%
for Qwen 3.5 4B, 20.8\% for 9B, and 32.3\% for 27B. Under the complete governed
configuration, it fell to 0\% for 4B, 0.2\% for 9B, and 0\% for 27B, while
utility increased for all three models. The principal WASP study evaluated
Qwen 3.5 27B on 84 browser tasks across three seeds under off, policy-only, and
full conditions, giving 252 episodes per condition. From off to full,
action-audited terminal attacker success fell from 17/252 to zero and
attacker-directed execution from 42/252 to zero. Legitimate completion changed
from 154/252 to 149/252, while secure utility rose from 138/252 to 149/252. In
the policy-only/full comparison, attacker-directed execution fell from 15/252
to zero at the same aggregate utility, and all 740 attributable sanitisation
removals in the full condition were confirmed.

Overall, the findings show that independently governing observations and
actions reduced evaluated violations while retaining useful task performance
under the tested conditions. The results are limited to the settings tested,
so they do not show that \systemname\ will prevent every attack, is ready for
deployment, or works effectively for human operators. Even so, they support a
clear architectural principle: safeguards within the model can provide one
layer of protection, while a separate runtime retains control over which
privileged effects are allowed. The browser agent can therefore continue
planning useful work without giving the model final control over execution.
\end{singlespace}
