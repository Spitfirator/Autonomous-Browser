\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Browser agents turn model outputs into actions with real consequences, but the
pages they interpret may contain attacker-supplied instructions. Untrusted
content can thus shape proposals for privileged execution. This dissertation
presents \systemname, an independent runtime-enforcement layer between a browser
agent and its executor. \systemname\ mediates observations before the model sees
them and evaluates proposed actions immediately before execution. It combines
post-verified sanitisation, deterministic policy and data-flow controls,
protected-input handling, and redacted audit records. When needed, it requests
scoped approval or pauses for a direct human step. A Chrome extension displays
redacted decisions and relays responses, while enforcement authority remains
in the runtime.

Across matched AgentDojo runs of 629 attacked tasks per condition,
baseline attacker success for Qwen 3.5 4B, 9B, and 27B was 32.8\%, 20.8\%, and
32.3\%; the complete configuration reduced these rates to 0\%, 0.2\%, and 0\%,
respectively, while utility rose for each model. Replicated WASP evaluation
tested browser-native transfer with Qwen 3.5 27B on 84 cases over three seeds
under off, policy-only, and full conditions (252 episodes each).
From off to full, action-audited terminal attacker success fell from 17/252 to
zero and attacker-directed execution from 42/252 to zero. Legitimate completion was
154/252 off and 149/252 full, while secure utility rose from 138/252 to 149/252.
With model-facing guidance held constant, the policy-only/full contrast tested
the configured sanitisation increment: attacker-directed execution fell from
15/252 to zero at unchanged utility, and all 740/740 attributable sanitisation
attempts produced verified removals.

In the evaluated configurations, model-facing guidance may influence proposals,
but authority over observations and privileged execution remains with the
runtime. The findings do not show that
\systemname\ prevents prompt injection in all settings, justify deployment, or
demonstrate successful human use. Within those limits, the results support the
central claim that independent runtime enforcement can substantially reduce
evaluated policy violations while retaining useful autonomy.
