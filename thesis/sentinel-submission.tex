\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
Browser agents turn model outputs into consequential actions, yet the pages
they interpret may also contain attacker-supplied instructions. This creates an
authority problem: the model must interpret untrusted content while deciding
whether it should influence privileged effects. This dissertation presents
\systemname, an independent enforcement layer between a browser agent and its
executor. \systemname\ governs observations before they reach the model and
evaluates proposed actions immediately before execution. When policy requires
operator authority, it requests narrow approval or pauses for a direct human
step. It combines post-verified sanitisation, origin-scoped taint,
deterministic policy and data-flow rules, protected-input handling, and
redacted audit. A Chrome extension provides the browser-facing control plane,
displaying redacted decisions and relaying scoped responses while enforcement
authority remains in the runtime.

Across three matched AgentDojo comparisons of 629 attacked tasks per condition,
baseline attacker success for Qwen 3.5 4B, 9B, and 27B was 32.8\%, 20.8\%, and
32.3\%; the complete governed configuration reduced it to 0\%, 0.2\%, and 0\%,
respectively, while increasing utility. Replicated browser-native WASP evidence
covered Qwen 3.5 27B on 84 tasks across three seeds under off, policy-only, and
full conditions (252 episodes each). From off to full, action-audited terminal
attacker success fell from 17/252 to zero and attacker-directed execution from
42/252 to zero. Legitimate completion changed from 154/252 to 149/252; secure
utility rose from 138/252 to 149/252. Against policy-only, full governance cut
attacker-directed execution from 15/252 to zero at unchanged aggregate utility;
all 740 attributable sanitisation removals were confirmed.

Overall, the results show that independent governance reduced evaluated
violations while retaining useful performance under the tested conditions.
They do not demonstrate universal prevention, justify deployment, or show how
well human operators would use the system. Instead, they support a narrower
principle: the agent can continue planning while a separate runtime governs
observations and privileged execution.
